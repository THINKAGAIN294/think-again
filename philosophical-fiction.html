STUPID KING

By R.P Mohapatra

STUPID KING! It is a beautiful morning in the royal palace. For the last 4 days the raj mahal has been being decorated. The 8th birthday of Prince Aryan Dev Varma, the only son of His Majesty the Lord of Suryanagari, King Maharaja Veerendra Dev Varma, is coming. The King is having a large feast for the people of his state along with some cultural programs and a spiritual congregation in the evening. The throne room of the king; There are ministers, king’s guards, advisers, up to 50 sipahi and the king on his throne present in the room. All the news, problems and complaints of the day are nearly heard in the court. **Guard:** A visitor, your grace, if allowed? **His Grace:** Let him in. The door opens, the visitor comes inside the courtroom, bows before the king. **His Grace:** From where, sir? **Visitor:** From a distant state of yours, my lord — Vaathi. **His Grace:** From Vaathi, you are! Beautiful country… and you are? **Visitor:** I am just a normal writer, my lord. Visit places to gain knowledge, write something and beg my leave. Honoured that I’m under your roof today. I am Laxman Dhwaja. **His Grace:** I am honoured too having you here. Visit our country, do what you do. Welcome, always. Tomorrow is the Name Day of my son. Some arrangements are made, please be my guest. **Visitor:** Yes, your grace. Suryanagari and Vaathi are very far apart from each other. These two states are very different in culture and tradition as the people of both states follow different religions. The only thing that is common between these states is the river “Bindhya” that flows across the two states. The people of Suryanagari pray to the goddess Bindhya Devi, the river god in their religion. They worship nearly everything that is related to the river. There is a custom of not eating fish though they hunt for deer and ducks. On the other hand, people in Vaathi do not believe in goddess Bindhya though the river flows in their state. They worship a god “Tosa”, the god of lands who blesses everyone with fruits, vegetables, trees, and water. They believe the river is daughter of Tosa as it comes from and floats on the land. --- **The Name Day of Prince** The morning programs were executed well. All the customs, traditional worships and river puja were done in the morning hours. Now is the time for the evening spiritual congregation. The biggest hall of the palace — all the ministers, servants, nearly 500 people, guests, guards and the king with his family are present. Some wise pandits from the state have come to discuss God as the prince wants to know a lot more about him. The curiosity of the little prince has led him to questions like: *Who is God? Where does he live? What does he eat?* Now the meeting starts and the most aged pandit begins: **Pandit:** “The one who made us, who made you, your tiny hands and palms, who made the birds, the sky, the land, the rivers — who made the whole world — is the almighty God. He made us all.” **Prince:** You must have seen him. You know a lot about him? **Pandit:** Yes I have, and you have also seen God! **Prince:** No, I have not. I don't remember anything like that. **Pandit:** Haven’t you ever seen the river, son? Have you not seen Bindhya Devi? **Prince:** I got it now, Pandit ji. Yes I have. But did our Bindhya Devi river make this world? Did she make us all? How can a river…? **Pandit:** Ha ha, curious son — yes. The mother has made us all. God has made us all. We pray to the river as it is where God lives. Where our mother lives. **Prince:** So, God stays in the river? **Pandit:** Yes, God stays in the river, in stone, up in the sky. God is present everywhere! **Prince:** Everywhere? But I thought God was one? **Pandit:** Ha ha… God is one, son. But he is present everywhere — like air. **Prince:** So, God is like air. Then God is not present inside the river! **Pandit:** Okay, as your hands, legs, and palms are small, so is your ability to understand complex things. God is not understandable to you. **Prince:** Is God understandable to you? **Pandit:** No son. No one in the world has ever understood God. **Prince:** If no one understands God, how do you know God stays everywhere? Have you ever seen him in actual — his physical body? Intending no offense, I’m seriously interested. **Pandit:** I understand son. I was this curious when I was your age. See, we do not need to see him to believe that he is everywhere. Like — have you ever seen ghee in milk? Ghee is present throughout the milk, still we can’t see it. Likewise, God is present everywhere, though you can’t see him. Yet we can feel him. **Prince:** Yeah, about that ghee thing… I never thought that way. Now I understand something. God is everywhere — in the river too? **Pandit:** In land too, son. God is present in every form. You can ask your visitor about this. People in his country worship the land form of God. **Prince:** Father, I want to ask something to our visitor guest. **King:** As you can see the curiosity of my son, would you mind answering some of his questions, Laxman? I request. **Visitor:** It’s alright, your grace. My pleasure. **Prince:** So, God is present in the land? **Visitor:** No. I don’t believe that God is in the land. *(Everyone in the room is shocked.)* **Prince:** What? Is God not present everywhere? **Visitor:** No. I don’t think so. I have not seen God anywhere. **Prince:** Then tell me, have you seen ghee in the milk? *(Though confused by the answer, everyone laughs after prince replies.)* **Visitor:** Listen, boy. What happens if I show your father a stone, claiming it becomes a diamond when poured in water in a full moon night, and I demand 100 gold coins for the stone. Would your father believe me without proof? Similarly, if I’m the first man to make ghee from milk, would you believe me saying ghee comes from milk without seeing the process? **Pandit:** Stay in your limits, Laxman. Want to fill child’s memory with sinful thoughts. Like you believe two plus two is four because your teacher said — we believe in God because our gurus said, our ancestors said, our holy books said. *(Everyone claps loudly.)* **Visitor:** We do not believe two plus two is four because our teacher said. We know two plus two is four. If you have 2 apples, then I give you 2 more apples, what do you think — you have 9 apples now? **Pandit:** I know your intention very well. You want to convert everyone to non-believers — to sinners. Devil speaks through your tongue. You will go to hell. Before the visitor speaks anything, the king intervenes: **King:** Enough! I don’t want a quarrel here on my son’s birthday. Pandit ji, Laxman is our guest. Is this how we treat guests? And Laxman — we do not blame others’ religion and we do not listen to anything against ours. Still, I think what you said was not against our religion. Your thoughts will help my son in terms of natural thinking. Now this is it. Let’s enjoy the feast. We can continue the discussion tomorrow. --- **After the Feast — Two Countrymen Talking:** **1:** Saw how that visitor tried to insult our religion — our belief. He is a mad man. Will go to hell. **2:** Yeah. **1:** The king is God’s avatar. His palace is God’s house to us. But that man insulted our God in there. **2:** But king says that man said nothing against our religion. **1:** He is a stupid king. Should have arrested that sinner. **2:** But you just said the king is the avatar of God…

– By R.P Mohapatra
